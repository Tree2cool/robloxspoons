<objective>
The client-side discipline gate: six named scans over three files Lune cannot load.

Purpose: `tests/card/renderer_discipline.spec.luau` says exactly what this kind of spec is worth, in
its own header — *"src/client/ui cannot be loaded by Lune ... this gates the DISCIPLINE rather than
the behaviour ... That is a weak kind of gate and it says so."* Copy that honesty. A scan cannot
prove a prompt appears on a phone (that is **M-19**) or that a camera is usable (**M-21**). What it
*can* prove is that the shipped text does not contain the three specific shapes that would break
HUNT-01 on the client side:

1. **A prompt or part created outside the reveal path**, or not destroyed on the hide — a visual that
   outlives its entitlement.
2. **A yield in the receive handler** — V-12 guarantees handler *start* order only, so a slow
   `SpoonVisible` overtaken by its `SpoonHidden` leaves a spoon rendered after the server hid it.
3. **A position or count the client keeps or derives** — a `{ id -> {x,y,z} }` table, a "last seen"
   record, or a decrement inferred from the event stream.

And one drift check that is genuinely strong rather than weak: **the client's handled-kind list and
`HuntRouting.KINDS` must be the same seven names.** `HuntRouting` is a pure module Lune *can* load, so
that half is real; only the client half is a scan.

Output: `tests/hunt/client_discipline.spec.luau`.
</objective>

**Two decisions this plan takes:**

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every handler-body scan uses a **two-anchor slice**, never a single `find` | The hunt's handlers are short and textually similar (`view:show(...)`, `view:hide(...)`). A single-anchor slice that overruns into the next handler makes the scan check the wrong code and pass for the wrong reason — and the round_end spec already paid for this lesson with its `ENDER`/`VIEW_HEAD` anchors |
| 2 | Needles are chosen **against the mutant**, not against the clean text, and every mutant is **run** | The repo's recurring failure. Three gates have been written, looked right, and could not fire; one because `player.Character` is a prefix of `player.CharacterAdded`. For each needle in this plan, state in a comment what the mutant looks like and why the needle cannot match it. Then run it |
