<objective>
The headless bot simulator (CARD-11): two modes, a six-category outcome taxonomy, eight metrics, and
a CI regression subset that runs inside the existing test step. It drives the REAL engine, so a
result about the simulator is a result about the shipped game.

Purpose: research proved the hard half of Gate G1 is a theorem, not a measurement — structural
unsatisfiability is unreachable while Rule 8 holds (§3.1, confirmed at 0 events in ~230,000 rounds).
What actually fails is **greedy-hoarding livelock**, and pure deterministic greed livelocks at a
non-zero rate at every pool in the grid.

**What changed, and what the simulator's job now is.** 02-CONTEXT.md amendment **A7** supersedes
A1's mechanism: the engine now carries an anti-livelock valve (plan 02-13) that forces a uniformly
random pass when the whole table returns to a state it has already been in. A1's auto-pass could
never have carried the gate — its firing rate is exactly the tail of the reaction-time distribution
past the pass deadline (2.86% at 1.0s, 0.21% at 1.5s, 0.019% at 2.0s, ~0 at 5s), and a 1.5-second
pass deadline is not an AFK mechanic, so at any realistic deadline the mechanism fires essentially
never and the gate would silently reduce to pure greedy. So the simulator's job changes from
"measure whether the population escapes" to three things:
1. **prove the valve FIRES when it is needed** — a deterministic cycle, not a statistic
2. **show stalls are zero with the valve enabled, and non-zero with it disabled** at the selected pool
3. **report the pure-greedy and auto-pass curves as margin evidence**, not as the verdict

**And the valve's firing rate becomes a bar, not just a number.** Measured at N=10 over 4,000 rounds
per cell: k=1,j=1 stalls 0.275% -> 0.000% with the valve firing in 0.28% of rounds; k=2,j=1
0.250% -> 0.000% firing in 0.25%; k=2,j=2 **21.6% -> 0.025% firing in 21.6% of rounds**. That last
row is the lesson: k=2,j=2 is a bad pool that needs rescuing in more than one round in five, and its
valve-on stall rate (0.025%) would sail past a naive zero-stall bar while its firing rate screams.
Zero stalls is therefore a measured property of a selected configuration and never an unconditional
property of the engine — do not write "guarantees zero stalls" anywhere in this plan's output.

Research §3.7 adds the occupancy bar: median time-to-first-completion provably cannot see a table
where nobody has anything to do, occupancy can, and it costs one counter.
Output: `Bots.luau`, `Simulator.luau`, and a CI subset whose own gate has been proven capable of
failing — including the mechanism checks, which FAIL the gate rather than reporting.
</objective>

**Mode: `Pacing`** (the G1-gated population, amendment A1). An event-driven schedule:
- Per-action reaction time is **lognormal with median 0.45s and sigma 0.42 in log space**:
  `t = 0.45 * math.exp(0.42 * z)` with `z` from Box-Muller on `rng:nextNumber()`. That puts ~90% of
  samples inside [0.25, 0.90], matching CONTEXT's "roughly 250-900ms" (research §11.4, assumption
  A3). **Do not clamp the distribution** — the tail is load-bearing, see below.
- A live seat with a legal action is scheduled at `now + reactionTime()`. A seat with no legal action
  is not scheduled; it becomes schedulable the moment a pass lands in its inbox.
- **The AFK auto-pass mechanic (CARD-16, as modelled).** When a seat reaches a hand of 5 at time
  `t0`, draw its reaction time `r`. If `r <= opts.autoPassSeconds` the seat passes its
  `Bots.greedyPass` choice at `t0 + r`. If `r > opts.autoPassSeconds` the seat has idled past the
  pass deadline, so the shipped mechanic fires: it passes a `Bots.randomPass` choice at
  `t0 + opts.autoPassSeconds`, and `autoPasses` increments.
  **Write this reasoning in the header, because the mechanism the gate rests on is NOT this one:**
  a livelocked greedy table is not idle — every seat is drawing and passing on schedule — so an
  auto-pass that fires on inaction fires exactly as often as the reaction-time tail crosses the
  deadline, and no more. Measured: 2.86% of actions at a 1.0s deadline, 1.67% at 1.1s, 0.21% at 1.5s,
  0.019% at 2.0s, 0.0003% at 3.0s and ~0 at 5s and beyond. The deviation cliff is near 0.03% per
  pass, so only deadlines around 1.0-1.5s clear it — and **a 1.5-second pass deadline is not an AFK
  mechanic**: CARD-16 auto-passes an idle player and benches them after repeated strikes, so at that
  deadline it would be striking ordinary players several times a round. At any deadline a real AFK
  feature would use, this mechanism fires essentially never. That is why amendment A7 moved the
  zero-stall job into the engine's valve and **demoted auto-pass to a secondary mitigation whose
  deadline is now free to take a realistic product value**. Its rate is still reported, as margin
  evidence. `autoPassSeconds = math.huge` disables it, which is one half of the pure-greedy worst
  case that must still be reported alongside the verdict (the other half is
  `antiLivelockValve = false`).
- The round ends at `now >= Round.ROUND_CAP_SECONDS` (300s), or when completions reach
  `seatCount - 1`, or when `liveCount == 0`.

**Mode: `Solvability`** (zero-latency, the theorem re-check and the worst-case curve). Strict
round-robin starting at the dealer; each seat takes its legal action when its turn comes and is
skipped when it has none; `greedyPass` always, `autoPassSeconds` ignored, `now` never advances.
- **Exact cycle detection, not a timeout.** Hash the state with `Invariants.hash` plus the
  round-robin cursor after every action and keep a seen-set; a repeat is a literal cycle in the
  state graph and the outcome is `Livelock` **with proof**. Clear the seen-set on every `Completed`
  event, because a completion changes the state space (research §11.4).
- Cycle detection is only sound where the dynamics are deterministic, which is why this mode is
  strict round-robin and why `Pacing` must rely on the cap instead. **Do not mix them.**
- A hard action cap of `opts.actionCap or SOLVABILITY_ACTION_CAP` (define
  `SOLVABILITY_ACTION_CAP = 100_000`) classifies as `CapReached` with `hitActionCap = true` rather
  than hanging. **Record `hitActionCap` separately from the time cap**: it is what makes a
  cap-artifact distinguishable from a true residual livelock, and plan 02-14 triages every such round
  by re-running it at ten times the cap. This matters because the valve-on residual measured at
  k=2,j=2 (0.025%, 1 round in 4,000) was produced under a 20,000-action cap and may be the cap
  binding rather than a livelock the valve could not break — and which of those it is decides whether
  the honest claim is "zero stalls" or "zero stalls for the selected pool".
- **With the valve enabled this mode will essentially never report `Livelock`**, because the valve
  fires at or before the point the cursor-aware detector would prove a cycle. That is the expected
  behaviour, not a broken detector: the `Livelock` classification exists to characterise the
  valve-DISABLED twin, which is where the margin evidence comes from.
- If no live seat has a legal action while `Invariants.circulation(state) >= 1`, `error()`: that is
  the liveness invariant and it means the ring is corrupt (research §5).

**`sabotage = "DestroyRemovedCards"`** — after each `SeatRemoved` event, delete the routed cards from
the successor's inbox and from the in-play counters. This is the Rule 8 violation, implemented HERE
and never in `src/`, so no shipped code path can destroy a card. It exists to prove the
satisfiability tripwire and the gate itself can fail: research §3.4 measured it firing
`UNSATISFIABLE` in **2.9-5.7%** of churned rounds against **0.0%** on the honest path.

**After every action, in both modes:** call `Invariants.satisfiable(state)` ALWAYS, and
`Invariants.check(state)` as well when `opts.identityCheck` is true (plus
`Invariants.circulation(state)` when it is false).

**Classify `Unsatisfiable` from `satisfiable(state) == false`, never from a `check` violation name.**
`check` returns the FIRST violation in a locked order and `circulation` is violation 4 while
`unsatisfiable` is violation 9, so a state whose cards were destroyed reports `"circulation"` and
`check` never reaches the satisfiability test. A simulator that waited for the name `"unsatisfiable"`
would classify every destroyed-card round as a conservation error instead and the sabotage proof
would be testing the wrong thing. **Do not reorder `check`'s violation list to make the name come
out** — that would make every genuine conservation bug surface as a stall statistic instead of an
error, which inverts the design of `Invariants.check` (plans 02-01 and 02-06 carry the same warning).

So: on `satisfiable(state) == false` with `liveCount >= 1`, return immediately with
`outcome = "Unsatisfiable"` and `violation = "satisfiable-false"`. On any `check` violation, `error()`
with the violation name and the seed — a conservation break is an engine bug, not a round outcome,
and must not be averaged into a stall rate.

**`identityCheck = false` for BOTH the CI subset and the sweeps.** Research §11.1 measured
**1,904 rounds/s counters-only against 1,099 with the full scan** — a 1.7x tax the subset's wall-time
budget (task 3) cannot absorb at any rung of its N ladder, and plan 02-10 already declines it for the
sweep citing the same paragraph. Plan 02-08's fuzz suite is where the full identity scan earns its
cost. Nothing the subset was measuring is lost: `Invariants.satisfiable` runs on EVERY action in both
settings and `Invariants.circulation` covers the cheap half of conservation, so the identity scan's
only unique contribution here would be catching a card-identity bug that 02-08 fuzzes for directly.
`identityCheck = true` stays on `SimOpts` as a deliberate diagnostic option, never a default.

**Valve metrics, every round.** Read `state.valve.firings`, `state.valve.armed` and
`state.valve.seenResets` into the `RoundResult` at the end of the round. `seenResets` is there
because `VALVE_SEEN_CAP` clears the WHOLE window on overflow, so a non-progressing trajectory whose
period exceeds the cap is never detected — the valve is blind there, not merely memory-bounded. It
exists in `ValveState` and in no result type today, which means plan 02-14 could label a round
`CapArtifact` or `ResidualLivelock` without being able to say "the valve was blind here". One field
makes the honest claim auditable, and it is exactly the k=2,j=2 0.025% row where the distinction
decides the headline sentence. These are first-class: a pool whose valve fires often is a bad
pool even at zero stalls (measured: k=2,j=2 fires in 21.6% of rounds while its valve-on stall rate is
only 0.025%, which a naive zero-stall bar would wave through). The simulator does not drive the valve
and must not reimplement it — it lives in the engine (plan 02-13) and the simulator only observes it
through `state.valve`.

**Outcome taxonomy — six mutually exclusive categories (research §7.7).** Evaluate in this order:
| order | outcome | condition | owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `DeadPool` | `Pool.validate` failed at setup | the pool config is invalid |
| 2 | `Unsatisfiable` | the §3.1 predicate fired | **an engine bug: a Rule 8 violation** |
| 3 | `RosterShortfall` | live seats < remaining spoons | Phase 6 lifecycle, **NOT a stall** |
| 4 | `Livelock` | Solvability mode: a state cycle was detected | bot model / pool |
| 5 | `CapReached` | the 300s cap or the action cap, with completions still possible | bot model / pool |
| 6 | `Cleared` | all remaining spoons claimed before the cap | — |
`RosterShortfall` must be its own category or the churn variant reports false G1 failures and the
gate gets tuned against a Phase 6 lifecycle matter (research Pitfall 8). Note in a comment that
`CapReached` as defined also closes a gap in CONTEXT's locked stall definition: "a round that hits
the cap with ZERO completions" does not catch the realistic failure of 6 of 8 spoons claimed and
then a livelock, and the multiset clause does not catch it either because the multiset is still
satisfiable. That refinement is inside CONTEXT's "precise structural-deadlock predicate" discretion,
not a change to the locked definition.

**Metrics, all eight (research §11.5):** median/p95 time-to-first-completion; median/p95
time-to-all-spoons; **mean seat occupancy** (sampled at EVERY action instant as the fraction of live
seats with a legal action — `#hand == 5`, or `#hand == 4` and `#inbox >= 1`); start-up ramp (time
until every seat has acted once); the outcome counts with the reproducing seed for every
non-`Cleared` round; dead-rank chasing (seat-instants holding >= 2 of a rank `r` with
`rankInPlay[r] + jokersInPlay < 4`); the auto-pass rate; and the deal-attempt mean and max.

**`runCell(opts, rounds)`** — runs `rounds` rounds with `seed = opts.seed + i`, aggregating into a
`CellResult` with the outcome counts, the medians and p95s, `occupancyMean`, `autoPassRate`,
`stallRate` (`(Unsatisfiable + Livelock + CapReached) / rounds` — `RosterShortfall` and `DeadPool`
EXCLUDED), and `failingSeeds` (the seed of every non-`Cleared`, non-`RosterShortfall` round, capped
at the first 20 so a broken cell does not print a thousand lines).

`CellResult` must also carry, because the bars and the mechanism checks read them and none may be
derived from a nil:
- `rounds`, and `firstCompletionSamples` (how many rounds contributed a time-to-first-completion).
  `medianFirst` and `p95First` are `nil` when that is 0, and `evaluateBars` fails `"no-completions"`
  rather than defaulting them.
- `valveFiringRate` (fraction of rounds with `valveFirings >= 1`), `valveFiringsMean`,
  `valveFiringsTotal`, `armedRate`, and `valveSeenResetsTotal` (the summed `valveSeenResets`)
- `capArtifactCandidates` — the seeds of rounds with `hitActionCap == true` (plan 02-14 triages them)
- `config.antiLivelockValve`, `k`, `j`, `jokerMode`, `n` and `autoPassSeconds`, so
  `evaluateMechanisms` can match a gated cell to its twin on the POOL without re-deriving it from a
  label
- `role: "gated" | "mechanism" | "autopass-probe" | "diagnostic"`, set by the caller and defaulting
  to `"gated"`. `evaluateMechanisms` reads it, and plan 02-10 already writes `role = "mechanism"`
  into its JSON, so make it a real `CellResult` field rather than a sweep-local annotation. A check
  that has to guess which cells are evidence and which are diagnostics is a check whose input set is
  undefined — and an undefined input set is precisely how a vacuity check ends up satisfied by the
  one cell the gate exists to reject (see task 3's cell-set paragraph)

**`evaluateBars(cell)`** — the four per-cell G1 bars, returning `(pass, failures)` where each failure
is a named string, never a bare boolean, and where **no bar is ever computed from a nil**:

0. `"no-completions"` — `cell.firstCompletionSamples == 0`. Fail here and return before touching any
   median. A cell in which nothing ever completed has `medianFirst == nil`, and both obvious
   handlings are wrong: `nil > 45` is a runtime error, and defaulting the nil to 0 makes a totally
   dead table PASS the pacing bar with a perfect score. Neither may happen, so the absence of
   completions is its own named failure.
1. `"stalls"` — `stallRate > 0`. The bar is ZERO stalls, not a low rate: one stalled round wastes
   five minutes for up to ten people, so a rate target would be accepting a known defect.
2. `"cleared-fraction"` — `cell.outcomes.Cleared < eligible` where
   `eligible = rounds - outcomes.DeadPool - outcomes.RosterShortfall`. This is the stalls bar derived
   from the other side: if `stallRate` is 0 then every eligible round cleared, so the two can only
   disagree if the outcome accounting is broken — which is worth catching for the same reason
   `Invariants` derives one side of conservation independently.
3. `"median-first"` (> 45s) and `"p95-first"` (> 120s).
4. `"occupancy"` — `occupancyMean < 0.60` (amendment A2 and research §3.7). Without this bar the
   selection rule picks `k=0, j=1`: **one single card circulating among nine seats**, a 14% occupancy
   table at 10 players that passes the other bars while being barely a game.
5. `"valve-rate"` — `valveFiringRate > VALVE_RATE_MAX`, with `VALVE_RATE_MAX = 0.01` (1% of rounds).
   Measured: good pools fire in 0.25-0.28% of rounds (4x margin below the bar) while k=2,j=2 fires in
   **21.6%** (20x above it). This bar is the one that catches the failure mode a zero-stall bar
   cannot see: k=2,j=2's valve-on stall rate is 0.025% and would look nearly fine, while the fact
   that the engine has to rescue more than one round in five says the pool is wrong. A pool whose
   valve fires often is a bad pool even at zero stalls.

**`evaluateMechanisms(cells, evidence)`** — the vacuity checks, as a property of a SET of cells plus
an explicit evidence record, returning `(pass, failures)` whose `pass` the caller MUST AND into its
verdict. Each failure names the bar it breaks; none of them is informational, and **none of them is
an exact-zero test**.

**Why `evidence` exists, and why this is the correction that matters most in this plan.** A threshold
is only a threshold *relative to a round count*. `VALVE_MIN_FIRINGS = 20` is calibrated for the
sweep: 540,000 search rounds at the measured 0.005 firings/round expect ~2,700 firings, a 135x
margin. Applied to the CI subset the same constant is a coin flip — at the same measured mean, 4,000
gated rounds expect 20.0 firings and the check fails about **47%** of runs, and 2,500 rounds expect
12.5 and it fails about **97%**. This plan already did that arithmetic once, for the per-cell case
("500 rounds expects 1.25 firings, so a per-cell `valveFirings >= 1` assertion would flake"), and
then applied the global constant across both round counts anyway. A gate that fails for a reason
unrelated to the thing it gates is a gate that gets deleted by whoever is on call. So: the
statistical thresholds belong to the SWEEP, CI proves the same facts DETERMINISTICALLY, and the
`evidence` record is where CI says which proof it is carrying.

Each valve bar is therefore an explicit either/or — deterministic evidence OR a statistic, never a
statistic borrowed across round counts:

- `"valve-never-fired"` — fails unless EITHER `evidence.deterministicFiringProven` is true, OR the
  summed `valveFirings` across cells with `config.antiLivelockValve == true` is at least
  `evidence.minFirings or VALVE_MIN_FIRINGS`, with `VALVE_MIN_FIRINGS = 20`. A gate that passes
  because the mechanism never ran is passing on pure greedy. The threshold is not `== 0` because at a
  0.25% firing rate an exact-zero test cannot fire in any short run — the defect this replaces — and
  it is not applied to CI because at CI's round count it is noise rather than a bar.
- `"valve-twin-missing"` — fails unless EITHER `evidence.deterministicTwinProven` is true, OR a cell
  with `config.antiLivelockValve == false` exists whose `(k, j, jokerMode)` matches **some** gated
  cell in the set.
  **The matching rule is SET-level and POOL-keyed, and it is stated here because the per-gated-cell
  reading is a landmine.** Read per gated cell, plan 02-10's confirm stage fails by construction: it
  emits 32 gated cells spanning N=3..10 against 4 twins, all of them at N=10. Since
  `evaluateMechanisms` is ANDed into the sweep CLI's exit code, that reading makes the sweep exit
  non-zero after a 20-to-40-minute run, every time, for a reason that is not a defect. `n` is
  deliberately NOT part of the match: the twin's job is to show the valve is load-bearing at a POOL,
  and the pool is `(k, j, jokerMode)`.
- `"valve-not-load-bearing"` — fails unless EITHER `evidence.deterministicTwinProven` is true, OR
  every matched valve-disabled twin has `stallRate >= VALVE_PROOF_MIN_STALL_RATE = 0.0005` (0.05%,
  five times below the measured 0.25%, so the check keeps real margin while still being a threshold).
  If the twin does not stall, either the pool never needed the valve or the twin was not actually
  running without it; both mean the mechanism is unproven at this pool.
- `"autopass-probe-missing"` — no cell in the set has `role == "autopass-probe"`. **This bar exists
  because without it `"autopass-never-fired"` is the one mechanism bar with no mutation case:** the
  natural implementation is "find the probe cell; if it is nil, skip", that passes silently, and so
  deleting the probe cell deletes the check. A missing probe is a FAILURE, not a skip.
- `"autopass-never-fired"` — a probe cell exists but its `autoPassRate` is below
  `AUTO_PASS_PROBE_MIN_RATE = 0.005`. Measured ~2.86% at a 1.0s deadline, so ~6x margin. Note why it
  is a PROBE and not a check on the gated cells: at a realistic AFK deadline the auto-pass correctly
  fires almost never, so requiring it to fire in the gated cells would be requiring the shipped
  product to be wrong.

**`evidence` is an input the CALLER must earn.** Both booleans are observations of a fixture run,
never literals: task 3 case 3 computes them from the `tests/harness/Livelock.luau` run and task 3
case 3a asserts that passing `{ deterministicFiringProven = false, deterministicTwinProven = false }`
with the CI cell set FAILS naming all three valve bars. An evidence field a caller could hardcode
`true` would be a check with an off switch, which is the same defect in a new place.

Write the reason for the shape in a comment: the first draft of this plan had an `autoPassRate == 0`
check that was both informational and an exact-zero test, which means it could never fire at a finite
deadline over hundreds of thousands of rounds. That is the same defect class Phase 1 verification
found twice. A mechanism check must (a) have a threshold with margin OR a deterministic proof,
calibrated to the round count it is actually applied at, (b) flip the `pass` boolean, and (c) have
its own mutation case that is reported as a FAILURE naming the bar. All five bars above have all
three; before this revision, two of them had only two.
