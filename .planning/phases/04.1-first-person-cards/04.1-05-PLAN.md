<objective>
The 2D layer catches up with the new coordinate contract, and the plate goes.

**The inset reading changes (F1).** `CardHud.readViewport` currently builds the viewport from
`cam.ViewportSize` and `GuiService:GetGuiInset()`. Both are wrong for this phase, and both are wrong
in the same direction: `ViewportSize` is the device safe area in UI offset units while FOV and the
render area are the **fullscreen** area, and `GetGuiInset` is the legacy top-bar semantic rather than
notch-aware `CoreUISafeInsets`. Two documented `GuiService:GetInsetArea` calls give the fullscreen
extent **and** all four insets, and plan 04 made the arithmetic a pure, tested function. This plan
makes the HUD call it and do nothing else.

**The plate goes (the disposition table).** Phase 4's `HandZone` was a 144-unit plate at the bottom of
the screen, and the dominant-colour plate behind it was what produced the hand/table split. **D-44
replaces the mechanism, not the intent:** the camera now produces that split. The owner's words were
"you would still see the people moving a little in the background, but the main focus is on your
cards", which is the same split reached by pointing a camera instead of by stacking two rectangles. So
`HandZone` keeps its name and its parenting role, loses its plate, and becomes the **hit layer**.

**One trap this plan exists to close.** `Projection` yields **physical fullscreen pixels**. The HUD's
`Root` carries a `UIScale`, so a child's offsets are in design units. A projected rect assigned
directly would be scaled a second time by the engine and every hit target would be wrong by a factor
of `scale` — on a 1080p desktop that is a factor of 2.97. **One helper, `CardHud.pxRect`, does the
subtract-origin-then-divide-by-scale conversion, once, and a scan asserts no other file does it.**

Output: the HUD on the new recipe, the plate removed, the conversion helper, and three new discipline
scans.
</objective>

**The viewport recipe, in one place, exactly:**

```lua
local function toMinMax(r: Rect)
    return { minX = r.Min.X, minY = r.Min.Y, maxX = r.Max.X, maxY = r.Max.Y }
end
local function readViewport(): Projection.Viewport
    local gs = game:GetService("GuiService")
    return Projection.viewportFrom(
        toMinMax(gs:GetInsetArea(Enum.ScreenInsets.None)),
        toMinMax(gs:GetInsetArea(Enum.ScreenInsets.CoreUISafeInsets))
    )
end
```

`readViewport` takes **no camera**. The camera is no longer consulted for geometry at all, which is
the point: FOV governs the fullscreen area and `GetInsetArea` reports it, so the HUD stops reading
`Camera.ViewportSize`. The `Camera.ViewportSize` **change signal** is still the re-solve trigger —
that is an event, not a measurement, and the header must say which it is so nobody "simplifies" the
measurement back in.
