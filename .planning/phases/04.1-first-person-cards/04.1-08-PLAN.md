<objective>
Success criterion 4 — "a player can still read how full the other seats are well enough to know
whether they are about to be one of the two who get nothing" — **cannot be met by the 3D stacks alone
under a fixed camera.** The 70 degree vertical FOV gives a horizontal FOV of 102 degrees at 16:9 and
87 degrees at 4:3, and with up to 10 seats most other stacks are simply **outside the frame**. The
deferred question from CONTEXT ("whether the stacks remain legible from a camera aimed at your own
hand") therefore has an answer: **no for off-frame seats, maybe for the two to four on-screen ones.**

So this plan adds the one thing the UI-SPEC added to the phase: a **depth rail**. One non-interactive
bar per other occupied seat, in ring order starting with the seat that receives from me, height
carrying the count, **no numerals, no names, no faces** — the Phase 4 rule that nothing numeric sits
over another seat is unchanged, and the rail shows only what a count already tells everyone (D-46's
own reasoning).

**This is the single place where this phase ADDS user interface, and it is Open Question 1.** It is
reversible by deleting one view and one pure module, at the cost of criterion 4 failing by design.
The plan says so, and the summary must repeat it, so the owner can overturn it by reading.

Three checker flags are fixed here:
- **F5** — the spec capped the rail at `min(count, 14)` while `Card.SEAT_STACK_CAP` is **16**, so the
  rail would saturate before the stacks it mirrors. The cap **is** the constant now, and the rail's
  height is derived from it rather than stored.
- **F6** — the ">= 2 px per extra card" claim was shown only at the 844x390 reference (2.2 px); at the
  0.85 scale floor a 2-unit step is **1.7 px**, below Phase 4's own rule. Fixed properly: the step is
  **3 design units**, so `3 * 0.85 = 2.55 px` clears the rule at the floor, and the rail's height
  becomes `3 * SEAT_STACK_CAP = 48` units — which pushes the callout stack down, which is why
  `Layout` is in this plan.
- **F7** — the bar colour was ambiguous between `PILE_PANEL` and `TEXT_ON_DARK`. **`TEXT_ON_DARK`,
  decided**: `PILE_PANEL` against the `PLATE` backing is about 1.4:1 and would fail a 3:1 non-text
  contrast check outright, besides reading as a card rather than as a gauge. The check is computed
  against the **blend** of the translucent plate over both scene extremes, which is the honest form of
  the question.

Output: the pure bar model, the layout slot it occupies, the view, and the gates.
</objective>

```luau
--!strict  -- src/shared/Card/Rail.luau   (PURE)
export type Bar = {
    seat: number,        -- the OTHER seat this bar stands for
    order: number,       -- 1 = the seat that receives from me (my right), then around the ring
    x: number, y: number, w: number, h: number,   -- design units, origin = the rail's own top-left
    saturated: boolean,  -- count >= SEAT_STACK_CAP, so the bar is at full height
}
Rail.BAR_W = 12
Rail.BAR_GAP = 4
Rail.STEP_UNITS = 3          -- F6: 3 * SCALE_MIN = 2.55 px, clears the ">= 2 px per card" rule
Rail.MAX_BARS = 9            -- 10-player cap minus me
Rail.TOP_UNITS = 4
Rail.heightUnits: () -> number      -- DERIVED: STEP_UNITS * Card.SEAT_STACK_CAP  (= 48)
Rail.widthUnits: (barCount: number) -> number   -- DERIVED: n*BAR_W + (n-1)*BAR_GAP
Rail.COLOR_TOKEN = "TEXT_ON_DARK"   -- F7, decided. A token name, not a Color3: this is a pure zone
Rail.BACKING_TOKEN = "PLATE"
-- depths is the server's public per-seat count list, seat-indexed, exactly as CardSeatsView
-- already receives it. localSeat is excluded. Bars are ordered from the seat that receives from me.
Rail.bars: (depths: { number }, localSeat: number, occupied: { number }) -> { Bar }
```

**The rail, specified.** Top centre of the safe rect, `y = TOP_UNITS` (4), `heightUnits()` tall
(48), one bar per **other occupied** seat in ring order starting with the seat that receives from me,
bar width 12, gap 4, at most 9 bars = 140 design units wide. Bar height =
`STEP_UNITS * min(count, Card.SEAT_STACK_CAP)` — **the cap is the constant, not a copy of it (F5)** —
so a full pile is a full-height bar and the rail saturates exactly when the 3D stack does.
**Shape is the carrier**, colour is `TEXT_ON_DARK` on a `PLATE` backing, and there is **no text at
all** (Open Question 6: no accessible text alternative is added, and the spec records that as a known
gap rather than pretending otherwise).

**`Layout` changes.** `DESIGN` gains nothing numeric for the rail: `Layout` requires `Rail` and uses
`Rail.heightUnits()` and `Rail.TOP_UNITS`. `Solution` gains `rail: Rect`, `elements` gains
`{ name = "rail", interactive = false }`, and the **callout stack is offset below the rail**:
`cy = railBottom + CALLOUT_GAP + (k-1) * (CALLOUT_H + CALLOUT_GAP)`. The spec's "callouts start at
y = 36" assumed a 32-high rail; with the F6 fix the rail is 48 high, so the callouts start at **56**
and the two-stack ends at **124** design units — still well inside the band (about 203 design units at
640x360), and now **derived** rather than two numbers that must be kept in agreement.
