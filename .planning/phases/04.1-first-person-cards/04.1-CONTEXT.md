## Phase Boundary

This phase replaces **how the local player's own hand is presented and touched**. Nothing else.

In scope:
- The camera during the card phase: locked, aimed at the seated player's own cards, with the table
  and the other seats visible beyond them.
- The local player's own cards as 3D objects held in front of them, with draw and pass motion.
- The hit model for those cards, which must still guarantee the 48 px floor.
- Whatever has to change in the status strip, the two pile affordances, the callouts and the
  flourish surface for them to still work in that view.

Explicitly NOT in scope:
- The card engine, the server card core, the gateway, the net layer, the seat hold, prediction and
  reconciliation, AFK auto-pass, the stall detector. **This phase replaces a renderer, not a game.**
- The hunt, the glint, the claim, scoring (Phase 5). The completion stub stays as it is.
- Round-end conditions, hider rotation, joiners and leavers (Phase 6).
- Deck skins and the deal animation (Phase 7, CARD-18 and SHOP-04). The seam `CardFlourish` already
  provides must survive; this phase does not build against it.
- Rigged arm and hand animation as authored assets. See the Deferred section: this phase moves the
  CARD, not the arms.

Requirements re-met (NOT newly met - Phase 4 met each against the 2D overlay this phase replaces):
CARD-02, CARD-13, CARD-15, CARD-17, CARD-19, UX-01, UX-02, UX-03, UX-04.

<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### Camera

- **D-41: The camera is locked for the whole time the local player is seated, and is released in
  the same frame their own `Completed` arrives.** D-30's ordering is unchanged and is not
  negotiable: overlay gone, controls back, THEN the flourish. The camera release joins the first
  group, not the third. A camera tween that outlives the release would hold a finisher still while
  their find-order score is already running, which is the exact harm D-30 exists to prevent.
- **D-42: The locked camera is set from the seat pose the server already sends, not from the
  character.** `CardTable.SeatSpec` carries position and look for every seat and the client already
  receives its own. Deriving the camera from the avatar's head would couple it to rig scale,
  respawn timing and the `PrimaryPart` race that cost a Studio session on 2026-11-10. The seat pose
  is data the client already trusts and is stable from the first frame of the round.
- **D-43: Field of view is FIXED for the card phase and restored on release.** This is the single
  assumption the whole phase rests on: a locked camera with a fixed FOV makes each card's projected
  screen rect a pure function of the viewport, which is what makes the 48 px floor assertable
  again. A user FOV setting, a dynamic FOV, or any camera shake would reintroduce exactly the
  objection D-26 raised against 3D cards. If anything later wants to animate the camera during the
  card phase, it is a BLOCKER to raise, not a tweak.
- **D-44: The camera aims so that the player's own cards occupy the lower portion of frame and the
  table with the other seats occupies the upper portion.** This is D-27's intent preserved through
  a different mechanism. The owner's words were "you would still see the people moving a little in
  the background, but the main focus is on your cards", which is the same split the 2D layout had,
  reached by pointing a camera instead of by stacking two rectangles.

### Cards, and the privacy rule that constrains them

- **D-45: The local player's own card objects are created ON THE OWNING CLIENT ONLY and are never
  server instances.** This is the hard one and it is not a style choice. Non-negotiable rule 3 says
  nothing hidden may be a replicated Instance, and CARD-13 says a player sees their own hand and
  only the pile DEPTHS of others. A card rendered as a server-side part in a player's hands is a
  replicated Instance carrying a hidden rank, readable by any cheat client in the game. The 2D
  overlay was private for free because a `ScreenGui` is client-side by construction; 3D objects are
  not, and this is the single largest new risk the phase introduces.
- **D-46: Other seats keep exactly what they have - count-driven stacks, no faces, no card objects.**
  CARD-13's privacy half is already proven by `privacy.spec`. This phase must not give another
  seat's hand a visual representation of any kind, not even a face-down card, because the number of
  objects is itself the count and the count is already public.
- **D-47: Hit testing stays 2D, computed by projecting the 3D cards.** Each card's screen rect comes
  from projecting its corners through the locked camera; an invisible button is positioned on that
  rect. The visuals become 3D and the hit model does not change at all. This keeps the Phase 4 hit
  model, its 48 px assertion and its `layout.spec` discipline intact, and it is why this phase is
  viable where D-26 was not. Raycasting against card geometry was considered and rejected: it makes
  the target size unassertable again, which is the thing being fixed.
- **D-48: Card faces keep the Phase 4 vocabulary unchanged** - `Faces`, the 50 identity keys, the
  `(rankGlyph, pipShape)` key, the greyscale rule and the machine-gated 4.5:1 luma floor. They are
  rendered onto a surface instead of into a `Frame`. The rendered SIZE changes, so M-14 is re-run;
  the vocabulary does not change, so M-14 is not re-designed.

### Motion

- **D-49: Motion is applied to the CARD, not to a rigged arm.** Draw and pass are tweens of the card
  object between the pile position, the fan and the destination. No animation assets, no rig
  changes, no `Animator` work. The owner asked for "hand movement animations"; this delivers the
  readable half of that with no new asset pipeline, and the rigged arms are deferred, named, and
  cheap to add later because the card's path is already the thing an arm would follow.
- **D-50: Motion never gates input, and the card leaves the hand model the instant the intent is
  sent.** CARD-14's prediction is unchanged: the local model updates immediately and the tween is
  decoration over it. A pass animation that had to finish before the next tap would destroy the
  tap-tap-tap scramble the game is built on, and would silently lengthen every pass by its own
  duration in a game scored in deciseconds.
- **D-51: One duration constant for card motion, in `Config/Card.luau`, beside the existing tween
  values.** Same reason D-32 gave one duration to every flourish: a per-path duration is how a
  presentation change becomes a pacing change nobody intended.

### What has to be re-proved

- **D-52: The 48 px floor is proved the same way it was before - by assertion, not by inspection.**
  The projected rect of every card at every supported viewport must measure >= 48 physical px, as a
  test. If the geometry cannot satisfy that at the smallest supported viewport, the CARD SIZE
  changes until it does. This is the criterion the whole phase was justified on and it is not
  allowed to degrade into "it looked fine on my monitor".
- **D-53: M-14 is re-run against the new render, on desk and on glass.** The desk half signed on
  2026-11-09 judged flat 72 x 92 faces and does not transfer. Spade versus club at the new rendered
  size is the pair to check first, as before.
- **D-54: Mobile cost is measured in this phase, not deferred to Phase 8.** Per-card 3D geometry or
  `ViewportFrame`s redrawn every frame is the one change here that can be slow on the low-end
  phones Phase 8 targets, and discovering it in Phase 8 would mean discovering it after Phases 5, 6
  and 7 have built on top of it.

### Claude's Discretion

Everything above is Claude's discretion under the owner's "do 4.1 then 5 overnight". The decisions
that most deserve a second look in the morning, in order:
1. **D-45** (client-only card objects) - correct but the largest new risk surface in the phase.
2. **D-49** (tween the card, not the arms) - the deliberate narrowing of "hand movement animations".
3. **D-43** (fixed FOV) - the assumption the 48 px guarantee rests on.

## Existing Code Insights

### Reusable assets
- `src/shared/Card/Faces.luau` - the 50-face vocabulary, pure, untouched by this phase. `PIP_SOURCE`
  is a single literal with a drift test, so a sprite escalation still works.
- `src/shared/Card/Layout.luau` - the design-unit solver. Its scale clamp and the 48 px reasoning
  are the precedent the new projected-rect geometry must match.
- `src/client/ui/CardTheme.luau` - every colour, font and stroke, with the luma contrast gate over
  it. Deliberately the single home so Phase 7 can reskin; this phase keeps that property.
- `src/shared/Card/Prediction.luau`, `Hints.luau`, `Controls.luau`, `Flourish.luau` - pure seams,
  all presentation-independent.

### Established patterns
- Pure core plus a thin Roblox adapter, everywhere. The 2026-11-10 seat bug is the cautionary case:
  the pure core was fully covered and the adapter had no test at all, so the bug lived in the seam.
  Any new adapter in this phase needs a gate even if it is only a source scan.
- Source-scan gates where Lune cannot load the module (`renderer_discipline.spec`,
  `service_wiring.spec`). They state their own weakness in the header.
- Mutation discipline: clean control, non-vacuity assertion, no-defect twin, and a mutation of the
  REAL source that must fire.

### Integration points
- `CardClient.luau` is the one wiring file on the client; `CardService` and `NetServer` are the
  server seam and are not touched.
- `CardSeatsView`, `CardCallout`, `CardFlourish`, `CardAudio` sit beside the hand and must keep
  working in the new view.
- `src/shared/Config/Card.luau` holds the timings; new constants go there, not inline.

## Deferred Ideas

- **Rigged arm and hand animation.** D-49 moves the card, not the arms. Authored animations are the
  natural follow-up and the card's path is already the trajectory an arm would follow, so this is
  additive rather than a rewrite. Owner: a later polish pass or Phase 8.
- **First-person view outside the card phase** (the hunt, the lobby). The owner said "the game
  should be first person", which may be broader than the card phase. This phase deliberately takes
  only the card phase, because that is the part that was discussed in full and the part where the
  48 px argument was resolved. The hunt's camera belongs to Phase 5 and should be decided there
  with the glint and line-of-sight model in hand, not pre-empted here.
- **Reading other seats' stacks from the new camera angle.** D-46 keeps the stacks as they are, but
  whether they remain legible from a camera aimed at your own hand is a real question this phase
  should answer and may hand to Phase 8 tuning.

</deferred>
