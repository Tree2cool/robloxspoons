<objective>
**The 48 px proof is this phase's whole justification.** Phase 4 decided D-26 against 3D cards
because a 3D card's on-screen size depends on camera framing, so the touch floor could not be
asserted. A locked camera with a fixed vertical FOV removes that objection: the projected rect of
every card becomes a pure function of the fullscreen height. This plan is where that stops being an
argument and becomes a test.

`src/shared/Card/Projection.luau` is **pure** — `src/shared/Card` is already a FOUND-06 pure zone,
so the purity gate comes free and is not optional. It implements the pinhole model directly and
**never calls `Camera:WorldToViewportPoint`**, for two reasons recorded in the spec: the docs leave
that call's origin ambiguous (fullscreen area versus device-safe area), and a pure model is the only
way the floor can be a headless assertion the way `Layout`'s already is. `WorldToScreenPoint` is the
other rejected alternative, for the same reason plus its inset behaviour (F13).

**The prototype numbers in the UI-SPEC came from an uncommitted Python script. This spec RE-DERIVES
them; it does not restate them.** Three independent derivations now agree on the geometry — the
researcher's (55.3 px worst-slot exposed width, ~100.4 px rect height at 640x360), the checker's
(card height fraction 0.2471, hit width ~55.9k), and the orchestrator's (56.2 px and 101.1 px). So
the test asserts the **floor** and **prints the measurement**, and treats a module derivation
outside the bracket as a BLOCKER rather than a number to nudge.

Output: the pure projection module and its spec.
</objective>

**`Projection` MUST NOT require `Layout`.** The dependency runs the other way: plan 04 makes
`Layout` require `Projection` (so the strip is positioned from the projected lifted-card top and no
caller passes a stale number), and a require in both directions is a cycle. `Projection` therefore
declares its own `Rect`/`Insets`/`Viewport` — structurally identical plain tables, so Luau's
structural typing keeps them interchangeable at every call site — and reads its own floors from
`Card.HAND` rather than from `Layout.DESIGN`. **Every number this module needs that Phase 4 kept in
`Layout.DESIGN` (the 48 px touch floor, the 72 x 92 card, `STEP`, `LIFT`, `INDEX_INSET_X`) is read
from `Card.HAND` or restated as a `Projection` constant with a one-line comment naming the
`Layout.DESIGN` key it must agree with; plan 04's `layout.spec` asserts the two agree.** Two numbers
that must match are gated, never assumed.

**THE TRANSFORM ORDER, STATED ONCE (checker F4). This is the contract; nothing may reorder it.**
Hand frame = `cameraCF * CFrame.new(HAND_OFFSET)`; all four steps are in hand-local studs, and the
module emits hand-local coordinates so the renderer applies one world transform and no arithmetic.

| # | Step | Exactly what |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Arc placement and roll** | `rho_s = (s - (n+1)/2) * ROLL_STEP_DEG`. Pivot = `(0, -R, 0)` with `R = STEP_STUDS / (2 sin(ROLL_STEP_DEG/2))`. Slot centre = `pivot + R * (sin rho, cos rho, 0)`; the card is rotated by `-rho` about the hand-local **Z** axis (the view axis). The ends therefore dip `R(1 - cos 8deg)` = 0.21 studs below the middle: a held-hand curve, not a flat row |
| 2 | **Lean** | rotate by `-LEAN_DEG` about the card's **own local X** axis (its right vector after step 1), **pivoting about the card's own CENTRE**. The top edge goes `(h/2) sin 12deg` farther from the camera and the bottom edge the same nearer; projected height foreshortens by `cos 12deg` **about the centre**. *Pivoting at the bottom edge instead would move the whole card up-screen and change the 0.930 H lowest-edge number — that is why the pivot is named* |
| 3 | **Lift** (selected slot only) | translate `+LIFT_STUDS` along the card's **own up vector**, i.e. after roll and lean. A world-up lift would unbalance the end cards, whose up vector is rolled by up to 8 degrees |
| 4 | **Depth step** | translate `+(s-1) * DEPTH_STEP_STUDS` along hand-local **+Z** (camera-ward; Roblox looks down -Z), applied **last** and along the hand axis rather than the card normal, so it is a pure translation that cannot shear the quad. Right neighbours end up nearer the camera |

**THE PINHOLE MODEL.** `fullW x fullH` is the **fullscreen area**, never `Camera.ViewportSize`:
verified that FOV and the render area are the fullscreen area while `ViewportSize` is the device safe
area in UI offset units. `tanHalf = tan(FOV/2)`; for a camera-space point with forward depth `z > 0`,
`sx = fullW/2 + (x/z)/tanHalf * fullH/2` and `sy = fullH/2 - (y/z)/tanHalf * fullH/2`; and
`k(fullH) = fullH / (2 * |OFFSET.Z| * tanHalf * 40)` px per design unit at the hand centre. Output is
in **fullscreen-origin** pixels. The conversion to the hit layer's coordinates is plan 04's
`viewportFrom` origin subtraction and happens nowhere else.

**THE HIT RECT IS INSCRIBED, NOT A BOUNDING BOX** (five steps, from the spec, in order): (1) project
slot `i`'s four corners at rest **and** at the lifted pose; (2) exposed polygon = slot `i`'s quad
minus everything right of slot `i+1`'s projected **left edge line** at rest — the last card is not
clipped; (3) vertical span = `[max(liftedTL.y, liftedTR.y), min(restBL.y, restBR.y)]`, which excludes
the slanted edges so every point inside the rect is on a real card; (4) horizontal span =
intersection, over that vertical span, of the exposed polygons at rest and lifted; (5) snap inward to
integers (left/top `ceil`, right/bottom `floor`). Consequences, all asserted: every tappable point is
on a card, so a tap between cards is a backdrop tap (D-04 intact); the 48 px assertion measures real
tappable area; neighbouring rects are **disjoint by construction**.
