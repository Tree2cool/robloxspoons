<objective>
The locked camera (D-41, D-42, D-43, D-44). This is the mechanism the whole phase rests on: a locked
camera with a fixed vertical FOV is what makes a card's projected rect a pure function of the
viewport, which is what makes the 48 px floor assertable and D-26's objection answerable.

Three decisions shape every line here.

**D-41 — release is in the first group, not the third.** D-30's order is unchanged and is not
negotiable: overlay gone, controls back, THEN the flourish. The camera release joins the *first*
group. A camera tween that outlived the release would hold a finisher still while their find-order
score is already running, which is the exact harm D-30 exists to prevent. The implementation makes
that structural rather than remembered: **`releaseCamera` is called inside `Controls.restore`**, and
`restore` is on every exit path there is — `onLocalCompleted` (before `startFlourish`), `SeatRemoved`,
`RoundEnd`, bench and disconnect all already go through it. **One release site, idempotent, no path
left out.** A second release site would be a second thing to forget.

**D-42 — the pose comes from the server's `SeatSpec`, never from the character.** Deriving the camera
from the avatar's head would couple it to rig scale, respawn timing and the `PrimaryPart` race that
cost a Studio session on 2026-11-10. `SeatSpec` carries position and look for every seat and the
client already receives its own.

**U10 / Open Question 10 is RESOLVED by reading the code, as the UI-SPEC required.** `look` is a
**direction**, not a target point: `CardService.luau:219-221` builds the hold CFrame as
`CFrame.lookAt(position, position + look)` and `CardSeatsView.luau:91-93` computes the stack anchor as
`position + look.Unit * SEAT_STACK_OFFSET_STUDS`. Both read it as a direction from the seat. The lock
uses the same reading, and **errors by name** if the horizontal component is degenerate rather than
guessing a yaw.

Output: the lock/release effects, the Roblox adapter, the wiring, and two gates.
</objective>

**The two orders, and why they are these orders.**

| `hide` | `restore` |
|---|---|
| 1. `setTouchControls(false)` — graphics first, so a Space cannot jump in the gap (existing reason, unchanged) | 1. **`releaseCamera()`** — the camera goes back before the controls do, because movement is camera-relative: restoring controls while a Scriptable camera still points at the table would send the finisher's first input in the table's direction instead of their own |
| 2. `bindSink()` | 2. `unbindSink()` — unbind first, or a still-bound sink eats the finisher's first key (existing reason, unchanged) |
| 3. **`lockCamera()`** — last, so there is no frame in which live movement controls meet a Scriptable camera | 3. `setTouchControls(true)` |

`onLocalCompleted` is **structurally unchanged**: `destroyOverlay()`, `restore()`, `startFlourish()`.
D-41 is satisfied because the release is inside `restore`, which is already in the first group and
already before the flourish. `restore` keeps its `hidden` guard, so release is idempotent.
